About the Dataset
PrimeKG is a precision medicine knowledge graph that integrates diseases, drugs, genes, phenotypes, and more into a single, computable network.

The dataset I’m using for this project is PrimeKG, developed by Chowdhury & Zitnik (2023) and made publicly available on the Harvard Dataverse under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Public Domain Dedication. This open license enables unrestricted use, modification, and sharing, which is ideal for open biomedical informatics research.

Citation: Chowdhury, S. N., & Zitnik, M. (2023). PrimeKG: A Precision Medicine Knowledge Graph. Harvard Dataverse. https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/IXA7BM

Dataset Overview

PrimeKG combines information from more than 20 biomedical databases and ontologies, linking over 17,000 diseases through more than 4 million relationships across drugs, genes, proteins, pathways, phenotypes, side effects, and clinical guideline text.

Why It Is Interesting

PrimeKG captures the biological complexity of disease in a way that computers can work with. It connects years of molecular biology, pharmacology, and clinical knowledge into a unified graph, enabling questions such as:

  • Which diseases share molecular or phenotypic mechanisms?
  • Where are there treatment gaps or repurposing opportunities?
  • How do biological mechanisms align (or fail to align) with what appears in clinical data?
Data Acquisition

PrimeKG was obtained directly from the official project page (PrimeKG Project) on the Harvard Dataverse. The dataset is distributed as CSV files and can be downloaded via HTTPS without authentication or API keys.

Data Cleaning & Preprocessing
  • Standardized all column names to lowercase to avoid case-sensitivity bugs.
  • Removed duplicate edges and duplicate node definitions.
  • Precomputed disease-neighbor sets for genes/proteins and phenotypes to speed up Jaccard similarity queries.
  • Normalized text for search (lowercased disease names) and added fuzzy suggestions for user input.
  • Handled missing or inconsistent entries conservatively by skipping them rather than guessing relationships.

Analyses in This Explorer
Disease Neighborhood Explorer

Visualizes all directly connected nodes for a given disease, grouped by type (drug, gene/protein, phenotype, etc.). Helps reveal how richly each disease is represented across biological and clinical dimensions.

Disease–Disease Similarity (Jaccard)

Computes similarity between diseases based on shared genes/proteins or shared phenotypes, surfacing potentially related conditions and shared mechanisms.

Drug Repurposing Candidates

Identifies drugs that treat phenotypically or genetically similar diseases but are not yet linked to the base disease in PrimeKG, providing interpretable repurposing hypotheses.
